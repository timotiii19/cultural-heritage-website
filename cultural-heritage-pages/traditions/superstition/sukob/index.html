02 — THE BELIEF

Sukob

When family milestones share a year.

A belief that certain weddings should not happen in the same year, placing family timing, mourning, and shared fortune in conversation.

Place of origin

The Philippines; the belief is described nationwide.

Date of origin

No documented date. The belief predates the 2006 horror film Sukob.

When it applies

Wedding planning in the same calendar year as a sibling’s wedding or a close family member’s death.

THE PRACTICE

The belief has two commonly described forms: siblings (sometimes first cousins) marrying in the same year, or a child marrying in the year a parent or grandparent dies. Both are said to bring misfortune to one or both marriages or the family.

THE MEANING

Some families understand sukob as blessings or prosperity being divided between weddings. It can express the importance of family harmony and respect for a period of mourning. The word is interpreted in different ways, including “sharing a cover” and “conflict”; neither reading alone explains the belief’s full history.

CONTEXT & CARE

Practical explanations have also been proposed: two weddings in one year may strain a family’s resources, and delaying a wedding may honor a period of mourning. These are interpretations, not a proven origin story. The 2006 film Sukob helped bring the belief to wider attention among younger audiences.

WHY IT STILL MATTERS
  • Some families reschedule weddings to avoid overlap; one USC informant described waiting until January after a sibling’s wedding.
  • A related belief says the eldest sibling should marry first.
  • The custom remains a personal or family decision, not a rule supported by evidence.
  • Pop culture keeps the belief visible: ABS-CBN reports the 2006 film is available on the Star Cinema YouTube channel; published box-office estimates are ₱186.41 million domestically and ₱203 million worldwide.
