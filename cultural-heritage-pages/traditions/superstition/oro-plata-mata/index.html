03 — THE BELIEF

Oro, Plata, Mata

A staircase counted in three.

A house-building belief turns each stair into a repeating count: gold, silver, death. The final step should land on oro or plata—not mata.

Place of origin

The Philippines, especially associated with Visayan house-building traditions in Cebu and Negros, with examples found nationwide.

Date of origin

No exact date. Some accounts connect the custom to Spanish-era building traditions.

When it is practiced

When building or buying a home, and when counting staircase steps.

THE PRACTICE

Count each stair in sequence: oro (gold), plata (silver), mata (death or bad luck), then begin again. The last step is ideally oro or plata. A related house-planning belief says the front entrance should face the rising sun.

01 oro
02 plata
03 mata
04 oro
05 plata

Five steps end on plata, a fortunate count. A sixth would land on mata.

THE MEANING

Oro means gold, plata means silver, and mata means death or misfortune. Ending on oro or plata is associated with good fortune and abundance; ending on mata is considered unlucky for the home and its residents.

CONTEXT & CARE

The precise beginning of the belief is uncertain. Sources connect it to Spanish-Filipino building traditions, while others note that centuries of trade may have brought multiple influences. Treat a single-origin explanation with care.

WHY IT STILL MATTERS
  • The belief makes folklore tangible: a repeating count can influence the design of an actual staircase.
  • The 1982 film Oro, Plata, Mata uses the three words as a story structure, following Negros families through wealth, decline, and misfortune.
  • A book titled Oro, Plata, Mata! Filipino Building Beliefs was published by the NCCA in 2000.
