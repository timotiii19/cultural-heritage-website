03 — THE TRADITION

Filipino Christmas Delicacies

The food of the season, from dawn Mass to midnight.

Native food practices and foreign influences combine in the dishes Filipinos share each December.

Place of origin

The Philippines, with influences from Spanish and other culinary traditions. Individual delicacies have different histories and origins.

Date of celebration

Primarily December, especially during Simbang Gabi, Christmas Eve, and Christmas Day.

HOW IT STARTED

Filipino Christmas delicacies developed through a combination of native food practices and foreign influences. Bibingka and puto bumbong are traditional rice-based delicacies that became closely associated with Simbang Gabi because people enjoyed them after dawn Mass. Hamon and queso de bola reflect European influences introduced through trade and colonization, while leche flan became a familiar dessert at Filipino celebrations.

MEANING / MESSAGE CONVEYED

These delicacies represent Filipino creativity, hospitality, cultural identity, and the joy of sharing food. Each dish can also bring back family memories and remind people of Christmas celebrations.

ESSENCE / IMPORTANCE / RELEVANCE

Filipino Christmas delicacies make the holiday season memorable and help preserve food traditions. They bring families and communities together through the familiar experience of eating bibingka and puto bumbong after Simbang Gabi and sharing festive dishes during Noche Buena.

FEATURED DELICACIES
01

Bibingka

A traditional Filipino rice cake made from rice flour or galapong and traditionally baked in banana leaves. It is commonly topped with salted egg, cheese, butter, sugar, and grated coconut.

Origin: Philippines; exact date of origin is unknown.
02

Puto Bumbong

A purple-colored Filipino sticky rice cake traditionally steamed inside bamboo tubes and served with butter, grated coconut, and brown sugar.

Origin: Philippines; associated with traditional rice-based food culture.
03

Queso de Bola

A round Edam cheese covered in red wax. It is a popular Christmas food and is often served with hamon, bread, and other festive dishes.

Origin: European cheese tradition; adopted into Filipino Christmas celebrations.
PHOTO NEEDED Hamon
04

Hamon

A cured or preserved ham, often glazed with brown sugar, pineapple, and spices. It is one of the recognizable centerpiece dishes during Noche Buena.

Origin: European/Spanish culinary influence.
05

Leche Flan

A rich and creamy caramel custard dessert made from egg yolks, milk, and sugar. It is commonly served during Christmas, birthdays, and other Filipino celebrations.

Origin: Spanish culinary influence; exact date of introduction is not established.
