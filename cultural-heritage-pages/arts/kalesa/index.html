FUNCTIONAL ART · CRAFT ON THE STREETS

KalesaA slower rhythm. A lasting impression.

Wood, wheels, a curved canopy, and the rhythm of a horse: the kalesa makes craftsmanship part of the streetscape. Look past the nostalgic silhouette and discover a vehicle shaped by skill, movement, and local memory.

A horse, a carriage, a journey.

The horse is trotting. Stop the animation whenever you like.

A carriage with many local stories.

Horse-drawn carriages became part of Philippine transport during the Spanish colonial period. Their story continued in towns where they carried passengers and goods before motor vehicles took over many everyday journeys. The National Library’s Going Places module documents this transport history.

The story is local as well as national. In Tuguegarao, the Philippine Information Agency describes the kalesa’s role in commuting, weddings, and community celebrations, and the craftsmanship highlighted by a Kalesa Spectacle in 2025. This is a working tradition remembered through the people who build, drive, and care for it.

Look at the kalesa as a relationship between craft and care. Maintaining the carriage matters, and so do the driver’s livelihood and the horse’s welfare. Cultural appreciation is richer when it recognizes everyone involved in keeping a tradition alive.

Structure you can see

Notice how the spokes connect the hub to the rim. The repeated lines give the wheel its visual rhythm while revealing its structural purpose. This is a useful object whose construction is part of its beauty.

A silhouette made for shelter

Trace the line of the canopy above the passenger seat. Its curve gives the carriage a recognizable profile and defines a small sheltered space within the open street.

The character of the carriage

Compare the painted body, trim, and visible fittings across the photographs. Decoration changes the character of the carriage without removing the practical demands of travel and maintenance.
